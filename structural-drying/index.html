Of all the stages in restoring water damage, drying the structure counts most, and it is the stage most often botched by people without training. Extraction takes care of standing water, but drywall, framing, subflooring, insulation, concrete and other building materials stay loaded with trapped moisture. Controlled evaporation and dehumidification, applied scientifically, are needed to get that moisture out before a wet structure develops mold, rotting wood, degraded materials and odors.

Livonia Restoration employs IICRC-certified structural drying technicians who understand psychrometry, the study of temperature, humidity and moisture relationships in air. That knowledge lets them work out where equipment goes, what temperature to hold and how air should move so evaporation is as effective as possible on every job. Different materials (drywall, hardwood, concrete, plaster) dry at different rates and need different approaches to reach acceptable moisture levels without causing additional damage.

Daily monitoring is built into how we dry a structure, and it relies on calibrated meters, thermal cameras and thermo-hygrometers. The result is a detailed log of readings that tracks how moisture comes down over time in each material that got wet. Equipment is adjusted daily based on these readings to optimize drying efficiency. A surface that feels dry to your hand proves nothing, so the job is finished only once all affected materials test within the acceptable standards set by IICRC S500.

Signs You Need Structural Drying in Livonia

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Condensation forming on windows in the area that was wet
  • Wallboard with water stains, puffiness or a spongy feel
  • Surfaces that stay cool or clammy once the standing water has been removed
  • Gaps opening up in a hardwood floor, or planks with raised edges or centers
  • Trapped moisture often announces itself with a persistent musty odor after cleanup
  • Humidity that will not come down in the affected space, whatever ventilation you try
  • Materials breaking down in a spot that got wet before and was not dried by professionals

Why Professional Structural Drying Matters

Structural drying is a science, not a guessing game. Secondary mold damage most often traces back to someone who put down fans and hoped things would dry. Professional structural drying requires understanding how different materials absorb and release moisture, calculating the grain depression (moisture removal capacity) each situation needs, and monitoring daily to confirm drying is progressing properly. Lacking that understanding, people either stop too soon and leave moisture for mold, or dry too hard and harm wood and other materials.

Professional drying equipment is not a bigger version of what stores sell, it is a different kind of machine. A commercial LGR dehumidifier, short for low-grain refrigerant, pulls 10 to 20 times as much moisture out of the air as a residential one, while high-velocity air movers set up the exact airflow that speeds evaporation off structural surfaces. Machines like these cost a great deal and take training to use well, which explains why professional results are so much better than a do-it-yourself effort.

Structural Drying Prevention Tips

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • The dehumidifiers and air movers should run around the clock until our technician takes them out
  • Address any water intrusion immediately, because the faster drying begins, the better the outcome
  • Moisture can sit deep in a material whose surface feels dry, so rely on the meter readings
  • If a new damp spot or smell shows up while drying is underway, let the technician know at once
  • Plan for daily access so readings can be taken and equipment adjusted
  • Dehumidifiers lose efficiency when outside air gets in, so keep drying areas closed up

Structural Drying, Step by Step

We follow a proven process to ensure every job is completed to the highest standards. Here's how we handle structural drying from start to finish:

1

Moisture Assessment

We build a complete moisture map of every affected area with hygrometers, thermal imaging and moisture meters. This map guides equipment placement and establishes baseline readings.

2

Equipment Deployment

Commercial LGR dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers are strategically positioned based on our moisture map. We tune airflow and temperature to suit the materials that need drying.

3

Daily Monitoring

Each day a technician visits to document progress, reposition equipment and measure moisture at every monitoring point. You receive updates on the drying timeline.

4

Verifying the Dry Standard

Once every affected material reads at an acceptable moisture level, we record the final numbers, pull the equipment and certify the property as meeting dry standard.
